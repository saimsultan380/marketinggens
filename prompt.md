# Website Content Brief — Lead Generation Agency

For: Developer + Content Writer
Audience: US local business owners (restaurants, ecommerce, insurance, and other local businesses) who want more customers, not a tech lesson.

**Core positioning:** "We don't sell marketing. We sell leads, appointments, and growth."
Every page should answer one question for the visitor: _"Will this get me more customers?"_ Never lead with tools, platforms, or technical methods — lead with outcomes. Technical detail (ads, SEO, automation, CRM, etc.) belongs in supporting bullet points only, after the benefit is stated.

## 2. INDUSTRIES HUB PAGE

Headline: "We Know Your Industry. We Know What Gets You Customers."
Intro: One sentence per industry, then cards to each dedicated page.
Note: For Medical & Healthcare, use "patients" instead of "customers" throughout — tone should be trustworthy and compliant-feeling, not salesy.

### 2a. Local Businesses

Headline: "Be the Business Your Neighborhood Calls First"
Subhead: "From home services to salons to gyms — we get your phone ringing and your calendar full."
Pain points to address: invisible on Google, losing customers to competitors nearby, no online reviews/reputation, no consistent lead flow.
CTA: "See If We Can Grow Your Business"

### 2b. Restaurants

Headline: "Full Tables. Every Night."
Subhead: "We bring new diners through your door and keep regulars coming back."
Pain points: empty tables on weekdays, no online ordering visibility, competitors dominating delivery apps and local search, no repeat-customer system.
CTA: "Get a Free Restaurant Growth Audit"

### 2c. Ecommerce

Headline: "Turn Browsers Into Buyers"
Subhead: "More traffic, more conversions, more repeat customers — without wasting ad spend."
Pain points: high ad spend with low ROI, cart abandonment, low repeat purchase rate, losing customers to bigger competitors.
CTA: "Find Out What's Costing You Sales"

### 2d. Insurance

Headline: "Qualified Leads. Ready to Talk."
Subhead: "We connect you with people actively looking for coverage — not cold names on a list."
Pain points: buying bad/shared leads, low appointment show-up rate, expensive cost-per-lead, no consistent pipeline.
CTA: "Get Better Leads, Not More Leads"

### 2e. Medical & Wellness Practices

_(Dentists, aesthetic/cosmetic clinics, private doctors' offices, old age homes / assisted living)_

Headline: "A Full Schedule of Patients Who Are Ready to Book"
Subhead: "We help medical and wellness practices attract new patients, fill appointment slots, and build lasting trust in your community."
Pain points to address: empty appointment gaps, patients choosing bigger clinics/chains over private practice, no online reputation or reviews, referrals alone aren't enough anymore, families struggling to find/trust a facility (for old age homes), low visibility for cash-pay/elective services like aesthetics.
Sub-notes for content writer (tone matters more here — keep it trustworthy, warm, never salesy; avoid anything that sounds like it's pressuring patients or promising medical outcomes):

- **Dentists:** "Fill your chair, not just your waiting room." Focus on new patient bookings, recall/cleaning reminders, reviews.
- **Aesthetics/Cosmetic Clinics:** "Turn interest into booked consultations." Focus on high-value elective service bookings, before/after trust-building, discretion.
- **Private Doctors:** "Be the practice your community trusts first." Focus on new patient intake, appointment reminders, local visibility.
- **Old Age Homes / Assisted Living:** "Help families find you when it matters most." Focus on inquiry calls/tours booked, trust and compassion in messaging, family decision-maker as the real audience — not the resident.
  CTA: "Get a Free Practice Growth Audit"

## GLOBAL NOTES FOR DEVELOPER

- Primary CTA everywhere: **"Get My Free Business Audit"** — should appear in header nav (as a button), after every major section, and in footer.
- Navigation: Home | Industries | Services | How It Works | Results | About | FAQ | Contact — with "Free Audit" as a standout button, separate from the nav list.
- Mobile-first: business owners will likely visit on phones. Forms must be short and thumb-friendly.
- Add trust signals near CTAs: certifications, client logos, "X businesses helped," Google review stars, etc.

## GLOBAL NOTES FOR CONTENT WRITER

- Write for a business owner reading at a 6th–8th grade level who is busy and skeptical of "marketing agencies."
- Every headline should imply an outcome (more customers, more sales, full calendar) — never a method (SEO, ads, funnels) in the headline itself.
- Keep paragraphs short (2–3 sentences max). Use bullet points over blocks of text.
- Tone: confident, plain-spoken, honest — not hypey or salesy. Avoid buzzwords like "synergy," "cutting-edge," "revolutionary."
